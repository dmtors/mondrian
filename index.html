Tap the colours, or drag along a street. The bigger the shape, the lower the note. On a keyboard, play 1–4, Q–R, A–F and Z–V, one row per size; − and + change the octave.

    How it works

    Four Wikipedia articles make the instrument. Each one shapes part of the sound with a trust signal: what the Wikimedia Attribution API says about how the article is written, sourced and read.

    • Waveform: the more people have written the article, the richer the tone, from a pure sine for a few writers to a sawtooth for a crowd.
    • Filter: the more references it cites, the brighter the sound.
    • Envelope: the more people read it (page views in the last 30 days), the longer each note rings.
    • Effect: the more recently it was updated, the more the note wobbles. A trending article shimmers.

    Each colour is a note. Yellow is the home note, and red, blue and grey are the notes that go with it, so there are no wrong notes. Turn Pitch to move them all up or down, turn Mood to change which notes go together, from dreamy to spooky, and press Octave to move everything higher or lower. The same note lives all over the painting, and its size picks the octave: the squares on the streets are highest, then small blocks, medium blocks and large blocks, an octave lower each. White is quiet. Drag along a street to play a run.

    The four article titles decide where the streets and blocks go and how the squares are coloured: the same articles always make the same painting.
